# Beginner Game Rules

From the book: Game One - Basic Maneuver and Fire

## 1. Sequence of Play

1. Each pilot selects the maneuver he will do and notes the mid-page turn number.
2. Pilots take shots where applicable.
3. Pilots exchange mid-turn numbers.
4. Pilots turn to the opponent's mid-turn page. (Disregard the image.)
5. Each pilot finds his selected maneuver on the mid-turn page for the final page number.
6. Pilots turn to final pages, noting tailing, and shots occur.
7. Pilots give clues (like tailing) and return to step 1.

## 2. Shooting in the Beginner's Game

Whenever a pilot begins step 2 with the opponent in his windscreen he shoots, doing damage to his opponent.  Note that it is entirely possible for both players to be shooting each other at the same time.

In the beginner's game, each shot at close range is worth 2 points of damage.  Each shot at medium range is worth 1 point of damage.  Each shot at long range is worth 1/2 point of damage.  6 or more points total, destroys a plane.  [Note: I've bumped this to 1, 2, 3 with 8 hit points per plane to avoid floating point numbers.]

If a plane is destroyed, its pilot is considered killed and the victor is awarded one victory point.

## 3. Tailing in the Beginner's Game

Whenever a player ends his turn on a page with a "T" printed on it, he is tailing his opponent.  This means that he is following behind and has an increased ability to react to his opponent's move.  It is not possible for both players to be tailing each other at the same time.

When a player is tailing his opponent, he may demand a clue.  The opponent must select his maneuver, and give a clue consisting of left, right or straight.

On each page the maneuvers are listed according to speed (for use in later games) and within each speed category the maneuvers are listed by direction.  The player being tailed must tell his opponent in what direction the maneuver he has chosen goes.  The tailing player then picks his own maneuver and play proceeds normally.

## 4. Getting Lost

If a pilot feels he is in danger of being shot down, he may wish to escape the field of battle, especially if he has previous veictories.  In addition, both pilots might simultaneously make wide-sweeping maneuvers which carry them away from each other.  In both cases these pilots might become lost to one another.

The two planes are lost to each other if-and-only-if the end-turn page that both pilots have is 223, or if both have a mid-turn page of 223.  Page 223 is a view of hte clouds that your apponent has disappeared behind.

When a plane gets lost, each pilot has two options: he may search or he may escape.

If both planes search, restart the battle on page 170.  Damage is carried over since this is a continuation.

If both pilots escape, the game is considered over and a draw.  A new game may be started, but planes are considered repaired, and different pilots may be substituted (if you are keeping statistics).

If one pilot escapes but the other searches, the searching pilot is considered to have driven his enemy from the sky, and scores 1/2 a victory.  A new battle may be begin, but planes are considered repaired, and different pilots may be substituted.  Remember, a pilot who fights and runs away will live to fight another day.

Note: pilots should write down and then simultaneously reveal whether they are searching or escaping when the situation arises, to preserve secrecy.

## 5. Winning the Beginner's Game

For the beginner's game, 6 points of damage destroys an aircraft.  The winning pilot scores 1 victory point.  If planes destroy each other in the same move (head-on shot) the game is a draw.  You score 1/2 victory point for driving your enemy from the sky.
