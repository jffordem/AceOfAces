# Intermediate Game Rules

The intermediate game introduces altitude and intermediate fire.   The use of guns to destroy the enemy plane is slightly more detailed.

Each plane has numbers listed next to the words 'climb' and 'dive'.  These numbers represent the number of feet which the plane may increase or decrease its altitude (in units of 25) in one turn.  This is the basic climb/dive number.

Each plane has a power dive and zoom climb number which are not used in the intermediate game.

Each plane has a gun column which tells what sort of guns it is armed with.  Next to that is the ammo number which is an abstract representation of how many rounds the gun may fire.

## 1. Sequence of Play (Intermediate Game)

1. Each pilot selects his maneuver, announces diving if applicable.
2. Pilots take shots.
3. Each pilot calls out the mid-term page number.
4. Each pilot goes to the other's mid-term page number.
5. Each pilot find his maneuver on the mid-turn page to lookup the end-term page.
6. Pilots turn to the end-term page, and not tailing and shots for the next turn.  Pilots may request knowledge of the enemy's altitude at this time.
7. Pilots give clues where applicable.

**Maneuvers with Bars**

This applies to the following maneuvers: 
- Stall
- Stall left
- Stall right
- Weave left-right
- Weave right-left
- Slip left
- Slip right
- Wing left
- Wing right

If a player chooses a stalling maneuver (any slow maneuver with a bar underneath the arrow) he must lose 100 ft of altitude in that turn.  He must always end the turn exactly 100 feet lower than he started it.

If a player chooses a slideslip, the bar has special meaning.  You must lose at least 100 feet, but you may lose up to your maximum dive amount.

If a player chooses the immleman turn, he must either climb his full climb ability or dive a number of feet equivalent to his climb ability.  For example, a plane capable of climbing 125 ft must climb or dive exactly 125 ft when doing an immleman turn.

If a player chooses a wingover he must remain level, losing or gaining no altitude at all.

**Diving and Pursuing**

If a pilot plans to make a noticeably steep dive of 200 feet or more in one turn, he must announce out loud 'diving'.  He may dive anywhere from 200 feet to his maximum, but not less than 200 feet once the announcement is made.  If the enemy pilot, upon hearing this, also desires to dive 200 feet or more, he may announce 'pursuing' and adjust his height record accordingly.

**Intermediate Game Shooting**

In the intermediate game, shooting takes on far more importance in relation to maneuvers and altitude.

Each plane is rated for type of gun and amount of ammo.  The gun table cross-indexes gun type with amount of ammo expended to yield amount of damage to the enemy.  In the intermediate game the shooter may withhold fire or fire a shot anywhere from 1 to 6 rounds long.  The longer the shot is, the more damage done, but more chance of jamming the guns and the longer the jam.

How to shoot:  
- Defender announce 'slow' where applicable and attacker figures combat bonus, taking height and range to target into account.
- Attacker checks eligibility for blast or blaze and announces the type of shot, and records ammo loss.
- Attacker rolls die.
- Result is read, damage is scored.  Check for james and critical hits.  In the case of head-on shot, the other player may become the attacker and repeat steps above.  Note: only one shot per plane per turn.

**Slow Bonus**

The following maneuvers are slow:  
- Stall left
- Turn left
- Weave left-right
- Stall
- Stall right
- Turn right
- Rotary turn
- Weave right-left

If the defender's maneuver is in the slow category on the page, the attacker adds one to his die roll for damage.

**Range to Target**

Range affects the accuracy of a shot by affecting the combat die roll.  A close range shot does not change the die roll.  A medium range shot subtracts 2 from the die roll.  A long range shot subtracts 4 from the roll.  All effects to the die roll are comulative.

Altitude affects shooting, by increasing the range to target.  An altitude difference of up to 175 ft is considered close.  An altitude difference of 200-375 ft is medium.  An altitude difference of 400-675 ft is long.  No shot may be taken at a plane which is more than 675 ft higher or lower than you.

Note: in those cases where the altitude difference and the range picture are in different categories, always use the longer one.  Thus, if planes are 250 ft distant by altitude, on a page where a close range picture, the range is medium.  If however the planes are 50 ft apart in height on a page with a long range picture, the range is long.

Note: all of these effects are summarized on the Gun Chart which appears in one of the foldout sheets.

If the modified die roll is less than 0, treat the result as 'miss'.  If the modified roll is over 7, treat the result as a 7.

Height advantage: if the attacker is more than 100 feet above his opponent, he gets +1 to his die roll for damage.

**Barrel Rolls**

As previously stated, a barrel roll is a difficult and tricky maneuver.  There is so much going on during a barrel roll that a pilot who has plotted a barrel roll in step 1 of a turn may not shoot in step 2 of that turn.

**Blast and Blaze Eligibility**

The attacker may only use blast (5 ammo points) or blaze (6 ammo points) under special conditions.  To use a blast, he must have plotted a straight (fast or cruising) in step 1 of a turn.  To blaze, the attacker must not only plot a straight for the upcoming turn (in step 1) but he must also have plotted a straight *last* turn.  Only if both these conditions are met may the attacker blaze.

**Ammo Restrictions**

Each plane has listed with it a certain amount of ammunition for its guns.  Each type of shot subtracts from the amount of ammo listed next to it from this total.  If a plane does not have enough ammo left to make a particular shot, he may not make it.  When the ammo reaches zero, the guns are empty and may not be fired.

**Gun Table Results**

Roll the die.  Find the proper table for the type of gun (single, double, unsync) and cross-index the die roll wiht the amount of ammo expended.  Add any bonuses for slow or height.  The result is a number, possibly followed by a "C" or a "+".  This is the total amount of damage done to the enemy plane.
